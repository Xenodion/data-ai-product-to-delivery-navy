## Session 2 — the cost of error
- Which cost-of-error line is a **legal or reputational** risk, not just money? **A wrong status, amount or deadline stated to a merchant on a dispute or a PSD2 / KYC question** (Inputs row 31 in `02_sizing.xlsx`, ≈ €250 per error on money alone, analogy: Mastercard 2025, $128 per chargeback). The merchant acts on it (misses a chargeback deadline, re-ships, waits) and can complain to the regulator; payment-service providers have information and complaint-handling duties under PSD2. As in Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149), the company answers for what its tools tell customers, even though a human sends the reply here. This is why dispute tickets are **excluded from drafting in year 1** (context only), and why the copilot never states a status, amount or date that is not backed by a linked transaction (S1, question 4). Payout errors (≈ €120 each, guess) are mainly money: extra contacts, goodwill credits, a churn tail.
- Who in the company **owns** that risk? **The Head of Compliance** owns the legal exposure: the PSD2 / KYC wording the copilot may flag, and the decision to bring disputes back into scope. **The Head of Support** owns the day-to-day quality of replies and the switch back to "context only" (S1, question 3). Our team, as product owner, owns the measurement: the uncaught-wrong-reply rate (bar: ≤ 0.5 % of drafts) reported on every eval run from S4.
