## 2 · Decide when to stop — the stop criterion
*"We stop improving **misrouting among the tickets routed automatically** at **2.7 %** because **the time saved stops responding to it** — it is already under the 8 % the sizing assumes (`Flow!D27`), each further avoided misroute is worth one €3 hand-over (`Flow!D28`), and the 13.32 % flip point (`Decision!D57`) does not depend on it — and the next point would cost a new hand-labelled set and a retraining run of the Qualifier."*

- **Component metric and value:** misrouting among auto-routed tickets, 1 of 37 = 2.7 % (`evals/report_v0.md`).
- **The level-above metric that stops responding:** total time saved (level 4). Routing already gives 15 % × 74 % × 80 % ≈ 8.9 % of handling time (`Flow!D6`, `Flow!D8`, report), above the sizing's 6.6 %; what still moves the time saved is the drafting cut, which must reach about 12.6 % of find-and-write time for the total to reach 13.32 % ((13.32 % − 8.9 %) ÷ (45 % × 78 % right first time), `Flow!D11`, report) — and no draft is grounded yet.
- **Cost of the next point:** new labels and a Qualifier retraining run; the effort goes instead to valid outputs (88 % against 98.5 %) and to the drafter's evidence (lookup, retrieval), the misses 1 and 3 of the report.
- **Floors, not stops (any miss blocks the release):** 0 urgent tickets auto-routed to the wrong desk — met (0 of 8); 0 drafts on tickets that belong to a person — **not met (4 of 4)**: blocked until the v2 line of `spec.md` §4 is built.

## 3 · Choose what to protect — the hierarchy, by segment
The primary is the product promise of the brief: **less hands-on time per ticket, at a quality no lower than today**. It is the metric most at risk of being gamed — an agent can go faster by sending drafts unread — so each guardrail and counter-metric below closes one way of winning it wrongly.

- **Segments reported** (from the S3 data audit, `governance.md` Session 3): language (French · English · other) · desk (payments · disputes · technical / API · compliance · Re-router) · ticket difficulty (clean · hard: vague wording, odd reference, thread, injection) · urgency (`high` · other) · agent seniority (new · experienced — the published gain concentrates on novices, `03_user_research.md`).
- **Fairness metrics, named and measured** (`evals/05_segment_fairness.ipynb` §2):
  - *Selection-rate parity* — does each language get auto-routed and draft-eligible (after the `human_only` gate) at the same rate? On the 100 hand-written cases (`eval_set_v1.csv`: `fr` = 40, `en` = 56, `other` = 4), auto-routing is **82.5 %** in French (`33 / 40`, 95 % CI `[68 %, 91 %]`) vs **75.0 %** in English (`42 / 56`, `[62 %, 84 %]`, Fisher `p = 0.458`), and draft eligibility after the `human_only` barrier is **72.5 %** (`29 / 40`) vs **66.1 %** (`37 / 56`, `p = 0.656`). On the 610 frozen synthetic cases (`test_natural`: `fr` = 309, `en` = 291), parity is within one point (`82.2 %` vs `83.2 %` auto-routed; `71.2 %` vs `70.1 %` draft-eligible).
  - *Equal error rates across segments* — on `eval_set_v1.csv`, misrouting among auto-routed tickets is **12.1 %** in French (`4 / 33`) vs **11.9 %** in English (`5 / 42`), silent errors are **15.0 %** (`6 / 40`) vs **10.7 %** (`6 / 56`, `p = 0.548`), while English abstains twice as often on routable tickets (`10.7 %` needless triage vs `5.0 %` in French). On `test_natural`, all 4 silent errors (`4 / 309 = 1.3 %`, including the `1 / 88` urgent wrong-desk) fall in French (`0 / 291` in English). Across desks on `eval_set_v1.csv`, `payments` has **0 %** silent errors (`0 / 25`, `92 %` desk accuracy) whereas `disputes` (`4 / 25 = 16.0 %`, `1 / 6` urgent wrong-desk) and `OTHER`/`UNDEFINED` tickets (`5 / 18 = 27.8 %` routed instead of abstaining) concentrate the risk.
- **Harm threshold** that stops the rollout, per segment, with its owner *(copied into `governance.md`, Session 5)*: if, for any language or desk, the **repeat-contact rate within 7 days** is higher in copilot-on weeks than in copilot-off weeks for two consecutive weeks, or the **Re-router correction rate** rises above today's misroute rate (1 in 7, `Flow!D26`), drafting drops to "context only" (no draft) on that segment until the gap is explained. Owner: Head of Support, with the Head of Compliance for disputes and compliance; reviewed monthly.
- **How it could be gamed → the repair:** agents send drafts unread to cut handling time → the QA-score guardrail, the "validated unread" counter-metric and seeded wrong drafts catch it; tickets are closed before the issue is solved → the 7-day repeat-contact counter-metric catches it; hard tickets are pushed to the Re-router to keep the error rate low → selection-rate parity by segment and the share of tickets handed over catch it.

The tree (component → product → business):
- Qualifier valid outputs (88 %) and abstention → **share routed automatically (74 %)** → reading-and-routing time −8.9 % → **agent cost per ticket** ↓ → net annual value (`Decision!D17`).
- Routing Node, misrouting 2.7 % → **hand-overs avoided** (€3 each) → ≥ €36,531 in year 1.
- Lookup + retrieval + Drafter (not built: no grounded draft) → drafting cut, needs ≥ 12.6 % → total time saved ≥ 13.32 % → the copilot beats the next-best project (`Decision!D57`).
- Validator (a person on every draft) → **uncaught serious errors unchanged** → no harm to merchants (non-effect).

## 5 · Make the judge trustworthy — the evaluation plan
- **Eval-set v1:** `evals/eval_set_v1.csv`, 100 cases (200 in production), stratified by language, desk, urgency and difficulty, with disputes near a deadline oversampled; inputs drafted with an AI assistant and tagged in every row, labels written by hand before any run. The 30-case sample fixed in `evals/README.md` is the golden set that never changes.
- **Grading guideline:** `evals/README.md` — verdicts PASS / FAIL / ESCALATE, six binary criteria with boundary examples, written on 13 November before anyone graded.
- **Two graders on the same 30 rows** (Yoni and Nahel, alone, `evals/grading/grader_A.csv` and `grader_B.csv`): **agreement on the verdict = 24 / 30 = 80 %**; Cohen's kappa 0.33, because almost every draft fails, chance alone would give about 70 %. Both graders would send 2 of 30 drafts as is (NAVY-EV-20, EV-29, two French clarifications). The disagreements: all six are 'FAIL' for one grader and 'PASS' for the other: five on C5 — when the merchant gives a transaction reference, is asking for the amount or currency 'asking for something already given'? — and one on C6 — does a missing greeting or sign-off make a draft unsendable? The guideline does not settle either boundary.
- **Judge:** ChatGPT (OpenAI, free tier, default model of 14 November 2026; the exact version was not shown and is not pinned, so a later rerun may use another model), a different family from the drafter (`mistralai/mistral-nemo`), with the prompt of `evals/judge_prompt.txt` — the closed rubric of the optional Judge, node 11 of flow v2 — on the same 30 rows with the same guideline (`evals/grading/keelpay-judge-results.csv`). **Human–judge agreement = 23 / 24 = 95.8 %** on the cases where the two graders agreed. Known biases: it is more lenient than the humans — it passed NAVY-EV-06, which both failed, and took the lenient side on all six human disagreements; it graded the 30 rows in one request rather than one at a time. Re-aligned on 14 November 2026.
- **The one fix, after both rates (14 November 2026):** the boundaries of C1, C5 and C6 clarified in the guideline and in the judge prompt — a draft that assumes one of two options the merchant asks about, asking for the amount or currency of a payment whose reference is given, or a draft without greeting or sign-off is a NO. The two rates above stay as measured; the clarified rules apply from the next grading round.
- **Cadence:** eval-set + judge at every change of model, prompt, rule or threshold; human QA sample weekly.
- *Optional, not graded* — red rows: `evals/adversarial_v0.csv`; assertions: `evals/promptfooconfig.yaml`.
